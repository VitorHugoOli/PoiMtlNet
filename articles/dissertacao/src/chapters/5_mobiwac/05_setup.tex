\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:mobiwac:setup-data}
Table~\ref{tab:mobiwac:datasets} summarizes our six datasets. Five come from Gowalla, a location-based social network, covering the U.S. states of Alabama, Arizona, Florida, Texas, and California
\cite{cho2011gowalla}; the sixth is Istanbul, from the Massive-STEPS collection

\subsection{Windows, splitting, and the integrity of the representation}
\label{sec:mobiwac:setup-windows}
\emph{Windows.} For each user with at least ten visits, we build time-ordered overlapping sliding windows of nine visits, one starting at each visit, with the next visit as the target, giving both tasks more examples. Near the end of a user's history, every start position within the last nine visits yields a shorter, padded window whose target is the same final visit; we keep only the full-length window ending there and drop these padded duplicates. The resulting prediction horizon is short but heavy-tailed: the median time from the last visit in a window to its target ranges from 0.4 hours in Florida to 5.5 hours in Istanbul, while 5 to 27 percent of targets lie over 3 days ahead.

\emph{Splitting.} We split by user with stratified five-fold cross-validation, so all of a user's windows fall in the same fold and overlap cannot leak: a test user's visits never appear in training. The held-out fold is the validation data; we reserve no third split. The split is stratified on the next-category label because the seven-class task is imbalanced: Food is the majority class in every dataset, from about 25 percent of visits in Florida to 34 percent in Alabama (Table~\ref{tab:mobiwac:datasets}).

\emph{Integrity of the representation.} We train Check2HGI once on the whole dataset
and then provide the resulting representation to every dedicated and joint model. The
prediction models use user-disjoint folds, but the representation has already seen the
check-ins of all users. This transductive setup creates possible paths through which
information about a test visit could reach a model. The four checks below examine these
paths separately. They bound specific risks; they do not prove that the representation
contains no information from the test side.

\emph{Prediction targets.} The objective used to train the representation does not read
the next-category or next-region targets. It learns by distinguishing observed graph
neighborhoods from shuffled ones. This prevents direct supervision from the prediction
targets, but it does not make the graph free of category information. Each visit's
observed category is an input feature of its node
(Section~\ref{sec:mobiwac:method-rep}). The final check below examines whether graph
links can propagate this feature from a later visit to an earlier one.

\emph{Whole-dataset training.} To estimate the effect of training the representation
over all users, we rebuilt it once per fold using only that fold's training users. We
then compared these representations with the one trained on the whole dataset. At
Alabama, Arizona, and Florida, the differences range from $-0.33$ to $+0.01$ Acc@10
points for region and from $0.00$ to $+0.29$ macro-F1 points for category, all within
fold noise.

The two tasks have different coverage in this comparison. The region result measures
the region representation directly. The category result uses a POI-level proxy and
includes only validation examples whose input places also occur in training, which is
67 to 87 percent of the examples across the three datasets. It does not measure the
check-in-specific context or visits to places unseen in training. The comparison
therefore indicates little transductive inflation within its measured scope, rather
than ruling it out for every test visit.

\emph{Region-transition prior.} A separate component can carry information between
visits: a table that records how often one region follows another. An earlier version
built this table from the whole dataset and inflated region accuracy by 13 to 27 points.
The current region-transition prior is built separately for each fold from training data
only. The HMT-GRN baseline is the only model that uses it. Our joint and dedicated
models do not (Section~\ref{sec:mobiwac:setup-baselines}).

\emph{Links between consecutive visits.} In the Check2HGI graph, each visit node is
linked to the visit that follows it. Because category is a node input feature, the
vector of an earlier visit could absorb the category of the next one. We screened for
this channel during development by training a linear probe to predict the next category
from only the last vector in each input window. The screen compares each candidate with
a clean reference encoder; it does not use the label-history benchmark as its decision
threshold.

At Florida, the clean reference reaches $0.4090$ macro-F1 with standardized features
and $0.4074$ with raw features, on a zero-to-one scale. A residual variant in the lineage
of the released encoder remains at the same level, with $0.4197$ and $0.4182$,
respectively. An attention-based candidate reaches $0.4976$ and $0.4863$, exceeds the
reference by more than the screening margin, and was therefore disqualified.

The label-history benchmark provides a second reference for interpreting these scores.
It is the best macro-F1 reached by four specified predictors that use only the observed
category history of the input window, with no encoder involved. One repeats the last
category. The other three are balanced logistic models that use the last category, the
category counts in the window, or the category at each position. The benchmark is not an
upper bound: a different predictor using the same restricted information could score
higher.

The benchmark is $0.3617$ at Florida and ranges from $0.2800$ to $0.3617$ across the
five datasets for which the required inputs are available. At Florida, the clean
references are four to six points above it. Every candidate in the raw-feature screen
is also above it. One candidate, a relation-typed graph encoder, falls below the
benchmark on the standardized run ($0.3328$) but rises above it on the raw run
($0.4142$). This exception does not change the screening decision because candidates
are judged relative to the clean reference, not against an absolute score.

\emph{Limits of the screening audit.} The probe is linear, was run only at Florida with
one random initialization over five user-grouped folds, and evaluated ancestor builds
of Check2HGI rather than the build that produced the reported results. Passing this
screen therefore bounds only the information exposed by a linear read. It does not
establish what a nonlinear sequence model could recover.

Together, these checks support a bounded integrity claim. The prediction targets do not
supervise Check2HGI, and measured transductive inflation is small within the stated
coverage. The region-transition prior uses only training data from each fold. The
screening audit also rejected a candidate whose score exceeded the clean reference by
more than the screening margin. The limits stated above remain.

The baseline representations follow their own documented training scopes. HGI is
pre-trained once on the whole dataset, as Check2HGI is, whereas CTLE is trained
separately in each fold using training users only.

\subsection{Metrics and statistical tests}
\label{sec:mobiwac:setup-metrics}
For the category task, we report macro-averaged F1 (macro-F1), which averages the per-class F1 so each of the seven categories counts equally; its reference point is the majority-class floor, the macro-F1 of always predicting the most common category. For the region task, we report accuracy at ten
(Acc@10), the fraction of test visits whose true region appears among the model's ten highest-scoring guesses
(a visit whose true region is absent from that fold's training data counts as an error); its
reference point is the dedicated model.

A claimed gain and a claimed match require different tests, because a difference that fails to reach significance is not by itself evidence of a match. A written analysis plan, fixed during development and before any result was read, assigned one test to each task, with the two-point margin pinned there: \emph{superiority} for next-category, \emph{non-inferiority} for next-region. It assigned the tests per task, not per dataset, and did not cover next-region superiority, so the four next-region gains of Section~\ref{sec:mobiwac:results-part2} are secondary results outside it. The plan registered a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test; at four seeds its exact one-sided $p$ cannot fall below $0.0625$, so we report a paired $t$ on the per-seed means, with the 90\% confidence interval of the paired difference, and the registered test alongside it. Both, and this departure, are released with the code. A \emph{seed} is one complete repetition of the five-fold experiment: it sets the random initialization and the user partition, so each seed draws its own division of the users, and within a seed both models read the same folds; folds and seeds are the two axes of repetition. On every dataset, both models use four seeds ($4\times5=20$ measurements) and the tests pair the per-seed means ($n{=}4$), with a Holm correction~\cite{holm1979} across the six next-category comparisons and, separately, across the four next-region ones. The \emph{non-inferiority} claim is that the joint model is no worse than the dedicated model by more than a two-point margin; we test it with the two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure~\cite{lakens2017tost}. The two-point margin is fixed in advance as well: a mobility-aware service acts on which region will be busy, not on a single rank position (Section~\ref{sec:mobiwac:problem}), so a two-point shift in Acc@10 is below the granularity at which such a service would behave differently. The precision of the equivalence test is measured across these four partitions: the paired difference's standard deviation is 0.01 to 0.18 points across the datasets, and the intervals pass a margin as small as one point at Alabama and Arizona (Section~\ref{sec:mobiwac:results-part2}).

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:mobiwac:setup-baselines}
The comparisons play three roles. The first role is played by the per-task state of the art, on our data, under user-disjoint five-fold protocols. For next-category, this is POI-RGNN \cite{capanema2023poirgnn}, re-implemented from its published architecture and hyperparameters, and a Markov baseline that predicts the category that most often follows the recent ones (Markov-K, best order per dataset); for region, we also compute a first-order Markov floor over region transitions, under the same sliding-window protocol and fold splits as our models. For next-region, the primary comparison is HMT-GRN \cite{Lim2022}, an end-to-end recurrent model (trained as one piece from the raw check-ins) evaluated on the same data, folds, and initialization. We keep its shared multitask skeleton and add a region-transition prior built per fold from training data. We drop its graph components and hierarchical beam search, which exist to serve its next-place target, a target that we do not predict. The result is a region-native model (region is one of its own prediction targets), not a reproduction of the complete published system. We also run STAN \cite{luo2021stan}, re-implemented from its published architecture and trained from the raw check-ins with its own embeddings and sequence construction under one fixed configuration. We adapt its output layer to rank region candidates, spatial objects that still support its candidate-distance matching; we do not adapt it to category, where distance matching has no meaning. A ReHDM \cite{li2025rehdm} reference is reported under its own published protocol.

The second role is played by two representation controls that attribute the category gain to our specific design, not to contextualization in general or to extra features: CTLE \cite{lin2021ctle}, the closest prior contextual check-in embedding, run end to end at Florida and with frozen weights (no fine-tuning) at Alabama, Arizona, and Istanbul; and a feature-concatenation control appending raw per-visit features to a standard place embedding under the same model.

The third role is a comparison between the two ways of coupling the tasks. The dominant published coupling is the directed cascade, predicting the category first and conditioning the place prediction on it \cite{ye2013nextmove,yu2020catdm,huang2024cslsl}. We test the cascade inside our own model rather than re-implementing those systems: we remove the shared trunk, where the two streams exchange information, and feed the predicted category forward into the region pathway, keeping the representation, outputs, and training configuration unchanged. This isolates the coupling topology (chain versus parallel) at no additional parameter or compute cost: the chain form drops the shared trunk and adds a conditioning projection of about 1,000 parameters. A final control, the standard place-level embedding \cite{huang2023hgi}, isolates what the per-visit representation adds. All other choices, including the nine-visit window, were fixed during development; the full training configuration for every model is in the released code (footnote~\ref{fn:mobiwac:code}).
