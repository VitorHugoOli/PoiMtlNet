\chapter{Errata to the Reproduced Articles}
\label{apx:errata}

Chapters~\ref{ch:cbic} to~\ref{ch:mobiwac} reproduce the three articles of this
collection in a unified format. Purely structural changes of the re-typeset, such as
renumbered sections and labels, tables re-set without vertical rules, captions placed
above tables, one-column figure sizes, and the substitution of each article's textual
self-references by chapter self-references, are declared in
Section~\ref{sec:intro:organization} and are
not listed individually. This appendix lists every \emph{content} departure from the
published or submitted texts: corrections of defects known in the article records,
wording substitutions required by the dissertation's writing rules, and the
corrections applied in the global bibliography. Each article folder in the research
repository keeps the corresponding errata registry; the published records themselves
are not edited.

None of the three chapters reproduces the front matter of its article. The abstract and
the keyword list are omitted in all three, the Portuguese resumo of the CoUrb article is
omitted with them, and the acknowledgments sections of the CBIC and CoUrb articles are
omitted as well; the MobiWac manuscript carries none. Venue, pages, identifier, status,
and authorship are stated in each chapter's preface instead, and the acknowledgments
belong to the article records rather than to the chapter bodies.

Three elements of the published article were deliberately preserved rather than
corrected. The sentence stating that Nash-MTL ``consistently yielded a better
overall performance'' is reproduced verbatim; it predates a later finding about the
optimizer implementation, and the chapter preface carries the of-the-time caution
instead of an edit. A cost claim in the same subsection, that Nash-MTL ``requires only
two matrix-vector products per iteration'', is also preserved, and it is not supported by
the method's own paper: the phrase does not appear there, and both implementations we
examined run an iterative concave-convex procedure whose default is twenty passes, each a
convex solve, on top of one backward pass per task. The claim therefore understates the
optimizer's cost. It is named here rather than corrected because the correction would run
in this dissertation's own favour, and because the clause sits in the same sentence as the
gradient-scale correction in Table~\ref{tab:apx:cbic-errata}; recording both makes the
asymmetry visible instead of leaving a reader to wonder why one half of a sentence was
audited and the other was not.
The emphasis convention of the published category table, which
bolds the better of the joint and single-task models per row and never bolds the HMRM
baseline, is also preserved exactly; the new caption states the convention so the
bolding is not misread as ``best per row''.

\section{Article 3 (Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}, MobiWac 2026)}
\label{apx:errata:mobiwac}

One content correction was applied. The submitted manuscript describes the prior work
of Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic} (\cite{silva2025mtlnet}) as having studied the next-category
and next-region tasks and as having observed negative transfer between them. Both
statements are inaccurate against that work: Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic} pairs static category
classification with next-category prediction and contains no region task, and it
hypothesizes negative transfer to explain a parity null rather than observing it. The
two sentences (in the introduction and the related-work recap) were corrected to state
that the earlier work found no consistent multi-task advantage for the paired category
tasks, and that the present study is the one that introduces the region task. The
correction removes an internal contradiction with the chapter's own novelty claim and
does not alter any experimental result; it was applied both here and in the version of
record, and is recorded in that article's errata. Beyond it, every number and claim in
Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} reproduces the submitted text. The chapter also carries three
marked additions declared in the chapter source: the italic preface, a recap subsection
covering the artifacts of Chapters~\ref{ch:cbic} and~\ref{ch:courb}, and the restoration
of one figure that the submitted eight-page build had cut for space, whose four values
are stated in the reproduced prose in both versions. This chapter writes no new lead
sentences for its tables, since the submitted text already introduces each of them.

\section{Two extractions of the same Gowalla corpus}
\label{apx:errata:corpus}

Chapters~\ref{ch:cbic} and~\ref{ch:courb} report the Florida subset of Gowalla as
20{,}301 users, 65{,}009 POIs, and 990{,}518 check-ins, while Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}
reports 21{,}052 users, 76{,}544 POIs, and 1{,}407{,}034 check-ins for the same state
of the same public dataset. The two are different extractions of that state, not
different datasets and not a discrepancy between them: the first two chapters report the
corpus as the extraction pipeline of their time produced it, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} reports the current extraction.

The mechanism is the category mapping, not the raw data and not a different filtering
rule. The extraction assigns a category to each check-in from a mapping table and
discards the rows whose category does not map, so widening that table keeps rows that an
earlier run had dropped. The mapping files were extended about eleven months after the
earlier Florida extraction, and the places they added fall mostly in
\emph{Entertainment}, \emph{Outdoors}, and \emph{Travel}. A controlled comparison against
the working extraction of the earlier period confirms the direction: each of its POIs,
users, and check-ins reappears in the current extraction, which adds further ones. The
current pipeline therefore cannot reproduce the earlier counts, and the two sets of
figures are kept as each chapter reported them rather than retrofitted to one another.
The comparison is recorded in the research repository, in
\texttt{src\_utils/cbic\_recompute\_result.md}.
