  27| \chapter{Introduction}
  28| \label{ch:intro}
  29| 
  30| \section{Context and motivation}
  31| \label{sec:intro:context}
  32| 
  33| Location-based social networks let a person announce where they are: a
  34| \emph{check-in} records that a given user visited a given place, a point of interest
  35| (POI), at a given time. Such records now exist at large scale, and they carry more
  36| regularity than their noise suggests;
  37| an analysis of large-scale human traces estimated the potential predictability of an
  38| individual's next location at about 93 percent~\cite{song2010limits}. A system that
  39| anticipates two properties of the next visit, \emph{what kind of place} it will be and
  40| \emph{in which part of the city} it will happen, can act before the visit occurs: a
  41| recommender can rank candidates of the right type, a navigation or transit service can
  42| prepare for where the user is heading, and a platform can allocate attention and
  43| resources by area. These uses reach beyond mobility research itself. The study of
  44| human mobility informs urban planning, disease spreading, and pollution analysis,
  45| among other societal concerns~\cite{luca2021mobilitysurvey}, and place categories are
  46| the semantic characterization that location-based services rely on~\cite{Xu2023}. The
  47| field also imports its tools: part of the representation machinery used in this
  48| research, the spatial location encoders of the second study, was first validated on
  49| geospatial tasks such as species recognition and remote sensing
  50| classification~\cite{mai2023sphere2vecgeneralpurposelocationrepresentation,wu2024torchspatial}.
  51| 
  52| The two properties above are the two prediction tasks of this dissertation. The
  53| \emph{next category} task predicts the category of the next visited place, one of
  54| seven top-level classes. The \emph{next region} task predicts the official geographic
  55| unit of the next visit, a census tract in the United States or a mahalle in Istanbul,
  56| a target space that ranges from hundreds to several thousand classes per dataset. The
  57| exact \emph{next place} task, predicting the specific establishment, is a third and
  58| different problem; this dissertation does not address it, and Chapter~\ref{ch:fundamentals} keeps the
  59| three tasks formally distinct.
  60| 
  61| A fourth task also appears in this dissertation: the first two studies paired next
  62| category prediction with the static classification of a place's category, category
  63| classification, and Section~\ref{sec:intro:arc} explains why the final study replaced
  64| it. Next category and next region were chosen for what a mobility-aware service can
  65| act on, and both are established end targets in the literature on the way to the
  66| harder next-place problem.
  67| 
  68| Serving both predictions raises a natural engineering wish. The two tasks read the
  69| same trace of visits, so one model could learn them together, sharing what they have
  70| in common; this is multi-task learning (MTL)~\cite{caruana1997multitask}, and it has
  71| produced single networks that handle many vision tasks at
  72| once~\cite{kokkinos2016ubernet}, recurrent models that diagnose over a hundred
  73| clinical conditions simultaneously~\cite{lipton2015learning}, and language models
  74| fine-tuned across dozens of tasks described by
  75| instructions~\cite{wei2022finetuned}. The operational appeal is
  76| concrete: a single model to maintain, and a single forward pass that returns both
  77| predictions together, instead of two dedicated single-task models running side by
  78| side. The promise, however, is not automatic.
  79| % [NEEDS SIGN-OFF: raised v1 assembly, 2026-07-23 | gate L3 fix A-1. Original sentence was near-verbatim with Ch.5
  80| % ("one artifact to train, version, and deploy, and one forward pass whose single set
  81| % of inputs produces both answers at once"); reworded here so the paper chapter keeps
  82| % the original wording. Claim unchanged. Revert if you prefer the duplication.]
  83| Sharing parameters between tasks can hurt one of them, a failure mode known as
  84| negative transfer. Whether joint training helps for this pair of POI prediction
  85| tasks, and what the answer depends on, was unresolved when this research started.
  86| 
  87| \section{Research question and the arc of this dissertation}
  88| \label{sec:intro:arc}
  89| 
  90| This dissertation answers one question:
  91| 
  92| \begin{quote}
  93| \textbf{Does multi-task learning help point-of-interest prediction (next category and
  94| next region), and what does the answer depend on?}
  95| \end{quote}
  96| 
  97| The answer was not found in one step, and the journey is the contribution. This
  98| dissertation is a collection of three studies, and it presents them in the order they
  99| happened: a negative result, its diagnosis, and its resolution.
 100| 
 101| The first study (Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic}, published at CBIC 2025) built the first joint model of
 102| this research, MTLnet, from standard components: a place-level graph embedding as
 103| input and hard parameter sharing beneath the two task heads. It predicted place
 104| categories in two forms, the static category classification task and the sequential
 105| prediction of the next category. The joint model did not consistently outperform the
 106| two dedicated single-task models, and it cost more to train. The paper reported this
 107| null result as a finding, together with three candidate explanations, one of which
 108| pointed at the input representation.
 109| 
 110| The second study (Chapter~\ref{ch:courb}, published at CoUrb 2026) tested the representation
 111| explanation first, as the cheapest controlled test among the three: it kept the MTLnet
 112| architecture fixed and replaced only the input, a monolithic 64-dimensional place
 113| embedding, with decomposed spatial, temporal, and categorical encoders. Category
 114| performance rose sharply at every state tested. The diagnosis followed: at that stage
 115| of the research, the input representation, not the sharing architecture, was the
 116| bottleneck.
 117| 
 118| The third study (Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}, submitted to MobiWac 2026 and under review) acted on that
 119| diagnosis. Any place-level embedding assigns a place the same vector on every visit; a
 120| representation that cannot tell a weekday lunch from a Saturday night out at the same
 121| place is working against both tasks at once. That observation is the hypothesis the
 122| final study tests. It moves the representation to the check-in level: one vector per
 123| visit. Acting on another of the first study's candidate explanations, the
 124| restrictiveness of hard parameter sharing, it also redesigns the sharing topology,
 125| replacing the shared hidden layers with cross-attention between two task-specific
 126| streams. The change of representation also refines what a joint model should predict,
 127| since under a per-visit representation the static classification of places becomes a
 128| less natural fit than the sequential tasks, and the task pair settles on next category
 129| and next region. With both changes in place, one joint model finally outperforms both
 130| dedicated single-task models across the six datasets studied, five states of the
 131| United States and Istanbul: on the category task at all six, and on the region task at
 132| four of six, with statistical non-inferiority within a two-point margin (TOST) at the
 133| other two.
 134| 
 135| The task pair therefore evolved across the three studies, from static category
 136| classification plus next category in the first two to next category plus next region
 137| in the last, and this dissertation names that evolution plainly rather than narrating
 138| one fixed experiment. The arc it reports is a correction trail: each study revised
 139| what the previous one concluded, and the later chapters state precisely which earlier
 140| conclusions they supersede.

 142| \section{Objectives}
 143| \label{sec:intro:objectives}
 144| 
 145| The general objective of this dissertation is to determine whether multi-task learning
 146| helps point-of-interest prediction for the next category and next region tasks, and to
 147| identify what the answer depends on. Four specific objectives structure the work, one
 148| per chapter of the collection and its consolidation:
 149| 
 150| \begin{enumerate}
 151|   \item Investigate whether a standard hard-parameter-sharing joint model benefits its
 152|         two category tasks, static category classification and next category
 153|         prediction, relative to dedicated single-task models (Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic}).
 154|   \item Diagnose the role of the input representation in that result by decomposing
 155|         and enriching the input while holding the architecture fixed (Chapter~\ref{ch:courb}).
 156|   \item Design and validate a check-in-level representation and a joint model built on
 157|         it for next category and next region prediction (Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}).
 158|   \item Anchor the final answer to the research question in a leakage-guarded
 159|         statistical protocol, the user-disjoint cross-validation with paired
 160|         significance and non-inferiority testing of Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}, and consolidate the
 161|         evidence of the three studies under it (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}).
 162| \end{enumerate}
 163| 
 164| \section{Scope and assumptions}
 165| \label{sec:intro:scope}
 166| 
 167| The empirical scope is fixed by the data and the targets. The experiments draw on
 168| observational check-in traces from two sources: the Gowalla dataset restricted to five
 169| states of the United States (Alabama, Arizona, Florida, California, Texas) and the
 170| Istanbul portion of the Massive-STEPS benchmark. The prediction targets are the next category, over the
 171| seven-class taxonomy defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:fundamentals}, and the next region, over census tracts in
 172| the United States and mahalles in Istanbul. The exact next place is not predicted
 173| anywhere in this work. The joint model operates under a single-model constraint: one
 174| trained artifact whose one forward pass over one set of inputs yields both
 175| predictions. Design-time assumptions of this kind are stated here and kept separate
 176| from the evaluation-time limitations discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}.

 232| \section{Contributions}
 233| \label{sec:intro:contributions}
 234| 
 235| The contributions of this dissertation fall into four groups.
 236| 
 237| \begin{description}
 238|   \item[Theoretical.] The finding that the input representation, together with the
 239|         sharing topology built on it, dominates whether multi-task learning helps
 240|         these POI prediction tasks. The arc also yields a corrected view of MTL for
 241|         this task family: a null result under a place-level representation coexists
 242|         with a positive result under a check-in-level one.
 243|   \item[Software.] The MTLnet framework (Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic}), the check-in-level
 244|         representation and the joint model built on it (Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}), and the
 245|         reproducible training and evaluation pipelines behind the experiments of
 246|         Chapters~\ref{ch:cbic} and~\ref{ch:mobiwac}, with code repositories referenced in the respective
 247|         chapters.
 248|   \item[Empirical.] A six-dataset benchmark of joint versus dedicated models for next
 249|         category and next region prediction under user-disjoint cross-validation,
 250|         with twenty fitted models per configuration (four seeds, each a complete
 251|         repetition of the five-fold experiment with a different random initialization, over one
 252|         fixed set of five folds), paired significance tests on the four per-seed means, that is,
 253|         four paired observations, non-inferiority margins, and a leakage audit. All
 254|         four seeds reuse the same fold partition, so the reported intervals do not
 255|         cover uncertainty over resampled user splits.
 256|         % [NEEDS SIGN-OFF: raised round 4, 2026-07-26 | REV-014, 2026-07-26] "twenty repetitions per configuration
 257|         % (four seeds, five folds)" -> the 20 fits kept, the inferential unit (n = 4)
 258|         % named, plus the fixed-partition clause. Sources: GLOSSARY rows "paired
 259|         % superiority test" + "n = 20 (fitted models) and n = 4 (inferential unit)";
 260|         % stats_n20/RESULTS.md:65-67; STATISTICAL_PROTOCOL.md:187-190.
 261|   \item[Practical.] Evidence that one deployable model can serve both prediction
 262|         services at once, outperforming the dedicated models on the category task and
 263|         outperforming or remaining non-inferior to them on the region task, with the
 264|         operational simplicity of a single artifact and a single forward pass.
 265| \end{description}
