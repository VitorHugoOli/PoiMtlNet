\paragraph*{Errata: o controle de concatenacao (Cap.~5, p.~79).}
A frase corrigida le:

\begin{quote}
A feature-concatenation control adds the per-visit features to the place embedding and feeds the
result to the same single-task model. On the scale of Table~9, this control closes most of the gap
between the two representations: at Alabama it raises the place embedding by $1.73$ macro-F1 points
(paired over five folds, $p = 0.003$, all folds in the same direction), which is the whole
$1.56$-point gap, and at Arizona it raises it by $1.78$ points ($p < 0.001$), or $70$ percent of the
$2.56$-point gap. At Arizona the check-in-level representation still scores $0.78$ points above the
concatenation arm ($p = 0.03$); at Alabama the two are within $0.18$ points of each other
($p = 0.53$). The category gain therefore comes mostly from the per-visit features that the
representation reads, and not from the hierarchical structure that reads them. What Table~9
establishes is that the input representation is the dominant factor, which this control supports;
it does not establish which part of the representation carries the gain.
\end{quote}

\noindent
A frase substituida afirmava que o ganho vem da representacao hierarquica e nao de injecao de
features. Medido na mesma escala, e a injecao de features que carrega a maior parte do ganho de
categoria. A conclusao central do capitulo nao muda, porque ela e sobre a representacao de entrada
contra a arquitetura, e nao sobre qual componente da representacao contribui mais.
