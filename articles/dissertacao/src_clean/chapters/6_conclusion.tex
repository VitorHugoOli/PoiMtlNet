\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This dissertation examined whether multitask learning helps next-category and
next-region prediction and what determines the answer. The three studies provide a
sequence of results: MTLnet did not consistently outperform its dedicated models
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic}); a controlled comparison identified the input representation
as the bottleneck for that configuration (Chapter~\ref{ch:courb}); and a
check-in-level representation with a new sharing topology produced a joint model that
outperforms the dedicated model on next category at all six datasets and on next
region at four of them (Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}). At Alabama and Arizona, the joint
model matches the region model by being statistically non-inferior within a two-point
margin (TOST).

\section{Contributions by chapter}
\label{sec:conc:contributions}

Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic} contributes MTLnet and the initial negative result. The model
uses a place-level graph embedding and hard parameter sharing for static category
classification of POIs held out from classifier training and for next-category
prediction. It did not consistently outperform the
dedicated single-task models and required more training time. The study proposed three
possible explanations: dissimilar tasks, an input representation that did not serve
both tasks well, and restrictive hard sharing. Its conclusion applies to that
place-level, hard-sharing configuration. The later studies test and revise it rather
than treating it as a general verdict on MTL.

Chapter~\ref{ch:courb} tests the representation explanation while keeping MTLnet
fixed. Replacing the 64-dimensional place embedding with 192-dimensional spatial,
temporal, and categorical encoders increases category macro-F1 by 20.2 to 22.0
percentage points across the three states evaluated. This result identifies the input
as the bottleneck at that stage, but two limitations constrain its interpretation.
First, the reported range comes from the static task, whose representation determines
the target by construction, as recorded in Appendix~B of \extravolume. It is not
evidence for the sequential task. Second, the comparison is not width-matched. An
equal-dimension control would be needed to separate the encoders' semantic
specialization from the increase in input width. The sequential results nonetheless
motivate the test performed in Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}, where the input does not
determine the target.

Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} contributes Check2HGI and the final joint model. Check2HGI
assigns one vector to each visit, and the model combines task-specific streams through
a shared cross-attention trunk while retaining a private spatial path for next-region
prediction. Evaluation uses user-disjoint cross-validation with twenty fitted models
per configuration: four seeds over the same five folds. Paired tests on the four
per-seed means show that the joint model outperforms the dedicated model on next
category at all six datasets by 5.3 to 9.4 macro-F1 points. It also outperforms the
region model at Istanbul, Florida, Texas, and California and matches it at Alabama and
Arizona through statistical non-inferiority within a two-point margin (TOST).
The registered primary analysis for the region task was non-inferiority. The four
region-superiority findings are secondary results outside that plan and are reported
as such here rather than reclassified after the analysis.

\section{The consolidated answer}
\label{sec:conc:answer}

The answer to the research question is conditional. With a place-level embedding and
hard parameter sharing, MTL did not consistently outperform the dedicated models in
Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic}. With Check2HGI and cross-attention sharing, the joint model in
Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} outperforms the dedicated category model at all six datasets
and the dedicated region model at four. It matches the region model at Alabama and
Arizona through statistical non-inferiority within a two-point margin (TOST). The
evidence therefore ties the benefit of MTL to the representation and to the sharing
topology used with it.

The freeze control in Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} narrows this explanation. When the
region pathway is frozen, the category gain remains at Alabama, Arizona, and Florida.
The gain therefore does not require the region task to improve the category task
during joint training. It comes from a component available to the joint architecture
but absent from the dedicated model. The control does not identify that component. A
development ablation at Florida removed the cross-attention stack and changed
next-category macro-F1 by $-0.04 \pm 0.13$. That result came from an earlier
configuration in which the region head used a transition prior that is absent from
the reported models. Its record attributes the null change to compensation among
components. It does not show that the shared trunk has no effect, so this dissertation
does not use it to assign the gain to one architectural block.

A capacity-matched comparison tests whether model size alone explains the gain. This
frame-level analysis was run after the Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} manuscript was
submitted. At Alabama, the dedicated category model was widened from about 0.6
million to about 4.2 million parameters. Three training configurations produced
twenty fitted models each, for sixty in total. The strongest widened configuration
has mean macro-F1 of 56.16 with standard deviation 1.89, compared with 56.82 for the
dedicated model at its tuned width and 64.51 for the joint model. The California
experiment gives the same pattern. At hidden dimension 752, the widened model has
101.9 percent of the joint model's parameters. Its better training configuration
reaches mean macro-F1 of 69.88 with standard deviation 0.26 over twenty fitted models,
while the tuned narrow model reaches 70.60 with standard deviation 0.07. The widened
model is 0.66 points below the narrow model at Alabama and 0.72 points below it at
California. In both experiments, the best widened configuration uses a lower learning
rate than the narrow configuration, which shows that the comparison selected a
training setting for the wider model. Within these two datasets and width controls,
additional parameters do not recover the joint model's gain.

The gradient analysis provides a compatible result at a different level. During
development, on an earlier data preparation, mean gradient cosine was $+0.001$ over
four seeds on four Gowalla states, three of which are among the five reported here.
The measurement concerns directional conflict for this task pair and does not support
a general claim about MTL. Appendix~\ref{apx:cosine} evaluates the same quantity for
the final model over seven datasets and reaches the same conclusion from the broader
measurement. With the check-in-level representation, the gradients of the two
sequential tasks are essentially orthogonal. In this configuration, sharing stopped
hurting, and gradient-balancing methods had little directional conflict to correct.
A tuned fixed weighting was sufficient.

The negative and positive results therefore describe different configurations rather
than contradictory conclusions. The first shows that joint training is not
automatically beneficial. The final study shows one setting in which a check-in-level
representation and a different sharing topology make one joint model competitive
with or better than the dedicated models under the stated tests.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conc:limitations}

Six limitations define the scope of the conclusions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Data vintage.} The five state datasets come from
        Gowalla~\cite{cho2011gowalla}, and the extraction spans January 2009 to August
        2011. Mobility patterns, available places, and check-in behavior may have
        changed since that period.
  \item \textbf{Taxonomy coarseness.} Next-category prediction uses seven top-level
        classes. A finer taxonomy may change the effect of joint training.
  \item \textbf{Transductive representation.} Check2HGI is trained on the check-in
        graph of each dataset. It cannot represent unseen places or users without
        retraining.
  \item \textbf{No next-place task.} The experiments do not predict the exact next
        POI. Their conclusions apply only to next category and next region.
  \item \textbf{Geographic coverage.} Istanbul is the only dataset outside the United
        States, so the non-United-States evidence comes from one city.
  \item \textbf{Task-pair confound.} The representation and sharing topology change
        at the same stage as the task pair. Chapters~\ref{ch:cbic}
        and~\ref{ch:courb} combine static category classification with next-category
        prediction. Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac} combines two sequential targets,
        next-category and next-region prediction. No controlled ablation separates
        the effect of this task-pair change from the representation and topology
        changes in the final result. Chapter~\ref{ch:courb} controls the task pair for
        the representation diagnosis. The capacity-matched analysis also excludes
        width as an explanation only for the category task at two of the six datasets,
        at one width per dataset, and does not test depth. The final pair's greater
        similarity may still contribute to the size of the improvement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conc:future}

Each limitation leads to a corresponding experiment. Newer and denser traces would
test whether the findings extend beyond the Gowalla period (limitation~1). A
finer-grained category taxonomy would test the effect of a larger semantic output
space (limitation~2). An inductive version of Check2HGI could represent unseen places
and users without retraining (limitation~3). Adding exact next place as a third target
would connect the model to the main task in the mobility literature and test whether
category and region predictions can provide structure for it
(limitation~4). Additional cities outside the United States would broaden the
geographic evidence (limitation~5). Finally, training the Chapter~\ref{ch:mobiwac}
architecture on the task pair from Chapter~\ref{ch:cbic}, while keeping Check2HGI,
would isolate the contribution of the task-pair change (limitation~6).

\section{Final remarks}
\label{sec:conc:final}

The final model produces two predictions in one forward pass: the kind of place a
person will visit and the region in which the visit will occur. The dissertation also
records how that result was reached. A negative result with a place-level
representation led to a controlled diagnosis, and that diagnosis motivated a
check-in-level representation and a different sharing topology. The negative result
is therefore not separate from the contribution. It establishes the condition that
the later studies had to change and explains why the final model works under a
different configuration.
