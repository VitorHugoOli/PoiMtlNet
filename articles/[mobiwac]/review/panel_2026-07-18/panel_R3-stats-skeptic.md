# Review — R3 (Methods & Statistics)

**Paper:** "Predicting the Next Category and Region of a Visit: A Check-in-Level Multi-Task Study on Mobility Data" (MobiWac 2026, regular track)

---

## 1. SUMMARY

The paper studies joint prediction of the next visit's category (7 classes) and region (520–8,501 census-tract-scale classes) from LBSN check-ins, on five Gowalla U.S. states plus Istanbul. It first proposes Check2HGI, a check-in-level extension of hierarchical graph infomax that gives each visit its own contextual vector rather than one vector per place, and reports +28 to +40 macro-F1 on next-category over a place-level HGI embedding. It then trains a single dual-stream model (private encoders, bidirectional cross-attention trunk, private spatial path for region) and reports that it beats dedicated single-task category models everywhere (+5.3 to +9.4 macro-F1) and beats or is TOST-non-inferior (±2 pp) to dedicated region models, with a claimed pre-registered assignment of superiority vs. non-inferiority tests over n=4 seed-level pairings. The framing is that coarse category+region predictions are the actionable unit for mobility-aware services (caching, provisioning), though no service is built or evaluated.

## 3. STRENGTHS

1. **The leakage audit is the right instrument, and the authors clearly learned from being burned.** §V-B does three things I almost never see: (i) a *measured* per-fold rebuild of the representation quantifying what whole-dataset training passes to the test side (≤0.33 pp at AL/AZ/FL); (ii) an explicit confession that an earlier whole-dataset region-transition prior inflated region accuracy by 13–27 points, now built per fold and confined to a baseline; (iii) an epoch-selection sensitivity analysis (§VI-B: ≤0.06/0.11 pp) showing the joint-vs-dedicated comparison does not hinge on checkpoint choice. This is well above venue norm.
2. **User-disjoint grouping genuinely closes the sliding-window overlap channel.** Overlapping stride-1 windows leak across random splits; splitting by user (all of a user's windows in one fold) is the correct fix and is stated crisply (§V-B).
3. **Distinguishing "gain" from "match" with pre-assigned superiority vs. TOST non-inferiority tests** (§V-C) is methodologically literate; most papers would report a non-significant difference as equivalence.
4. **The mechanism controls are the right ones**: per-visit-averaged-to-place ablation (attributing 64–90% of the representation gain to context, not extra signal), feature-concat control, frozen-region-pathway control isolating why category improves, and an in-model cascade-vs-parallel rewiring rather than an apples-to-oranges reimplementation comparison (§V-D, §VI-B).
5. Code and both datasets are public; the writing is unusually plain and readable.

## 4. WEAKNESSES (ranked)

1. **The two datasets that carry the headline region-superiority claim (TX, CA) are exactly the two where the evidentiary chain is thinnest — and the paper contradicts itself about them.** Fig. 3's caption says "(TX and CA: one seed)" for the region gain, while §VI-B ("All six datasets are measured at four seeds") and the Table III footnote ("All joint and dedicated entries average four seeds over five folds") say n=4, and the TOST/superiority intervals at TX (+2.10 to +2.13) and CA (+2.19 to +2.21) are presented as n=4 paired CIs. One of these statements is wrong. Moreover, the per-fold-rebuild leakage audit (§V-B) was run only at AL/AZ/FL — the three datasets where the region result is a tie or +0.71 — and *not* at TX/CA, the only two datasets whose gains clear the ±2 pp margin. The one place the paper claims a practically meaningful region win is the one place neither the audit nor (per the caption) the full seed protocol demonstrably ran.
2. **No test/validation separation: "The held-out fold is the validation data; we reserve no third split" (§V-B).** Epoch selection, the 0.75/0.25 loss weights, and the dedicated models' per-dataset batch-size/LR tuning all consult the same folds that produce the reported numbers. The joint-vs-dedicated *deltas* are partially protected (both sides enjoy the bias, and tuning the dedicated ceiling is anti-conservative for the authors' claim), but every absolute number in Tables II–III is model-selection-biased, and the external-baseline comparisons inherit an asymmetry (were HMT-GRN/STAN also read at their own validation-selected best epoch on the reporting fold?).
3. **The statistical inference treats seeds as the unit of replication while holding the data split fixed.** Folds are identical across seeds, so the ±0.01–0.02 seed sd's in Table III (and the resulting 0.02-pp-wide CIs) capture initialization noise only; fold-to-fold variance (e.g., ±2.4 in Table II) is the dominant component and is never propagated into any interval. The weakest statistical sentence in the paper is §V-C: *"The equivalence is well powered: the paired difference has a small standard deviation (0.01 to 0.18 points across the datasets), so the procedure has power near 1.0 to declare equivalence when the true difference is near zero."* This is post-hoc power computed from the observed data, under a variance model that excludes the dominant variance source. Of course the CIs are narrow — you have removed almost all the randomness from the design. The correct statement is: near-certain equivalence *of re-initializations on this one split*, which is a much weaker claim than the prose implies.
4. **Asymmetric reading of sub-margin effects.** The ±2 pp margin is justified (§V-C) by arguing a 2-point Acc@10 shift is "below the granularity at which such a service would behave differently." By that same argument, the FL (+0.71) and Istanbul (+0.19) gains are service-irrelevant — yet the abstract and conclusion count them as "outperforms at four of the six," while the AL −0.41 deficit (whole CI below zero, i.e., a statistically significant loss) is styled a "match." Sub-margin gains are wins; sub-margin losses are ties. The defensible headline under the paper's own margin is: *matches at four, outperforms at two* — and see weakness #1 for those two.
5. **The a priori status of the test assignment is asserted, not evidenced — and its internal logic is inconsistent.** The stated mechanism ("region gain grows with region count") would predict non-inferiority for Istanbul (520 regions, the *fewest*), yet Istanbul was assigned superiority and passes by +0.19 with a CI of (+0.15, +0.23), while AL (1,109 regions) was assigned non-inferiority and indeed loses slightly. No pre-registration artifact, timestamp, or repository evidence is offered. As written, the assignment pattern is indistinguishable from one made after seeing seed-0 results.
6. **The representation comparison confounds transductivity and feature access.** Check2HGI and HGI are trained once on the whole dataset (transductive) while CTLE is built per fold on training users only (inductive) — a protocol asymmetry inside the very table meant to isolate the representation. Also, each visit's *category* is an input feature of its graph node (§IV-A), so the silhouette 0.57 / NN-purity 0.98 geometry (§VI-A) is close to definitionally guaranteed; the feature-concat control is the right answer to this, but it is reported prose-only, with no number anywhere.
7. **Baseline caveats accumulate at the large states.** HMT-GRN is a modified re-implementation (graph components and beam search removed, prior added), scored only on visits whose region appears in training (a *different, easier* metric than the authors' own, per the Table III footnote); STAN runs 4/5 folds at TX and 2/5 at CA at seed 0; ReHDM is single-seed at TX/CA under its own protocol. The conclusion's "above every external baseline … at all six datasets" is built on partial folds, single seeds, and non-matched protocols precisely where it matters.
8. **Several load-bearing controls have no numbers**: the feature-concat control, the 64–90% averaging ablation, the frozen-region-pathway control ("within 0.3"), and the gradient-correlation claim ("measured during development, not shown", §II-C). Table II is seed 0 only.

## 5. MUST-FIX BEFORE ACCEPTANCE

1. **Resolve the Fig. 3 caption vs. Table III/§VI-B contradiction on TX/CA seed counts.** If TX/CA region results are single-seed, the superiority-beyond-margin claims and their CIs must be withdrawn or re-run at n=4; if the caption is stale, fix it and state so.
2. **Run the per-fold-rebuild leakage audit at TX and CA** (or at minimum at one of them), since these are the only datasets whose region gains exceed the pre-registered margin. An audit certifying ≤0.33 pp at the three datasets where you claim ties does not certify the two where you claim wins.
3. **Restate the region headline symmetrically under the paper's own margin** (abstract, §VI-B, conclusion): supported-but-sub-margin gains at FL/Istanbul must not be aggregated with TX/CA into "outperforms at four of six" while a significant sub-margin deficit at AL is called a match.
4. **Qualify all interval/power statements as conditional on the fixed split** (fix the §V-C power sentence), and state explicitly the Holm family: which and how many comparisons, whether region superiority tests and TOSTs are included, and at what α.
5. **Provide evidence for the a priori test assignment** (registration, tagged commit, or documented date), and explain why Istanbul — fewest regions — was assigned superiority given the paper's own region-count rationale.

## 6. DETAILED COMMENTS

- §V-B: "The split is stratified on the next-category label" — stratification is defined per-window but the split is per-user; how users are assigned to folds to achieve window-level stratification is not described.
- §V-B integrity argument, first ground ("its training objective is label-free… never sees a next-category or next-region target") is weaker than it looks: consecutive-visit edges mean a test user's visit-t node is trained to be consistent with its neighborhood *including visit t+1 — the prediction target*. The label never enters, but the transition does. Your second ground (the measured rebuild) is what actually closes this; I would reorder the argument to lead with the measurement and drop the suggestion that label-freeness alone is protective. Also state *which* model (joint? dedicated? both?) the ≤0.33 pp audit was run on.
- §V-B: the audit "covers the visits whose places appear in training (67 to 87 percent)" — so up to a third of test visits sit outside the audit, acknowledged in §VII. Report the metric split by seen/unseen-place visits so the reader can bound the exposure.
- §V-B: the masked-reconstruction auxiliary uses "each place's aggregated category features" — aggregated over all visits including test users'? If so, say so; it is inside the audited perimeter but should be explicit.
- §V-C: "we use four seeds for both models (4 × 5 = 20 measurements) and pair the per-seed means (n=4)" — with df=3, a paired t at p<0.001 after Holm rests entirely on near-zero seed variance; a normality caveat or a sign-flip/permutation check would cost one sentence.
- §VI-A: CTLE "at its authors' defaults" — the paper's own dedicated models are tuned per dataset; an untuned control two points below even the place embedding invites the suspicion that CTLE was under-fit, and CTLE never receives category features at all. The missing cell is CTLE + the same per-visit features.
- §VI-A: CTLE "33.45 macro-F1 at its best epoch and 29.69 at its final one … below our 75.15" — 75.15 is Florida Table II seed 0; fine, but the two-band story ("+28 to +29 small, +37 to +40 large") is asserted from single-seed numbers.
- Table II footnote ("The matching place-level value at Alabama and Istanbul (26.56) is a coincidence of two independent runs") — good that you flagged it; it would otherwise look like a copy-paste error.
- Table III: AL joint region 69.70 vs dedicated 70.11 — the text calls this "a small but statistically significant deficit, still well within the two-point margin" (§VI-B). This is the honest sentence the abstract should inherit.
- Table III footnote: HMT-GRN "scored on visits whose region appears in training" vs. your model where such visits "count as an error" (§V-C) — this favors the baseline, which is the right direction, but say the coverage percentage so the reader can see how much easier its denominator is.
- §VI-B cascade test: the cascade "runs under the configuration tuned for the parallel model" — properly disclosed, but then "the simpler parallel structure loses nothing" is only supported at 4 of 6 datasets (AL/AZ/FL/Istanbul); TX/CA are silently absent from this control too. TX/CA are absent from every control in the paper (leakage audit, freeze control, cascade, shortlist-distance analysis). This pattern needs one honest sentence about compute limits.
- §VII: "ten regions of 8,501 contain the true next region 65.69 percent of the time, over 500 times better than picking ten at random" — 0.6569/(10/8501) ≈ 558, arithmetic checks out; and the authors correctly label the 3–8 km shortlist analysis "motivation, not a measured service result."
- Fig. 3: plotting +0.0 and −0.4 bars alongside +9.3 on one axis makes the region story visually unreadable; a broken axis or separate panel would help.
- Minor: text says AZ category gain "+9.35," Table III arithmetic gives +9.36; abstract "matching it (statistically, within two points) at the other two" glosses that one of the two is a significant deficit.

## 7. QUESTIONS TO THE AUTHORS

1. Fig. 3's caption states the TX and CA region gains come from one seed; §VI-B and Table III state four seeds with 90% CIs of width ~0.03 pp. Which is correct, and if single-seed, what happens to the TX/CA superiority-beyond-margin claims?
2. What evidence can you provide that the superiority/non-inferiority assignment predates the results, and why was Istanbul (520 regions — fewer than Alabama's 1,109, which you assigned non-inferiority) expected a priori to *outperform*, given your own region-count mechanism?
3. Can you run the per-fold representation rebuild at Texas or California, and report the region metric split by visits to seen vs. unseen places, so the transductivity exposure is bounded at the datasets where your only practically-significant region gains occur?
