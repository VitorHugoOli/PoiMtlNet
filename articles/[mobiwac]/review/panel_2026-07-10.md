# Full reviews


## Review 1: Senior advisor figure: Brazilian professor of urban computing and distributed systems, reviewing at the structural level (section contracts, narrative placement, claim anchoring)

**Score: 4 (weak accept)** — A disciplined, honest two-act study whose headline is well anchored and statistically serious, but whose mechanism story, discussion measurements, and conclusion all sit outside their proper homes; weak accept, and a clear accept after one restructuring pass.

**Summary:** The paper asks whether one model can predict two coarse properties of a user's next check-in, its category and its region, instead of the exact POI. It proceeds in two acts: first, a check-in-level extension of hierarchical graph infomax (one vector per visit, not per place) that lifts next-category macro-F1 by roughly +28 to +40 points over a place-level HGI embedding on six datasets (five Gowalla U.S. states plus Istanbul from Massive-STEPS); second, a single dual-stream model with a cross-attention trunk and a private spatial path that outperforms per-dataset-tuned dedicated category models everywhere (+5.3 to +9.4 macro-F1) and outperforms or statistically matches (TOST, plus-or-minus 2 pp) the dedicated region model, with the region gain growing with the number of regions (Texas and California flagged as single-seed provisional). The statistical protocol is unusually disciplined for this literature: superiority and non-inferiority are tested with different instruments and each verb is bound to its test, a train-users-only leakage audit is reported, and the honest cost paragraph admits the joint model is larger than the two dedicated models combined. The mobility-management payoff is motivation only; no service is built or measured, which the paper states plainly.

**Strengths:**
- Claim discipline of a kind rarely seen at this level of venue: superiority (paired tests, Holm) and non-inferiority (TOST with an a-priori justified 2-pp margin) are kept separate and each verb is bound to its test; single-seed TX/CA cells are flagged provisional consistently in the abstract, the contributions, Table III, Fig. 4, and the limitations; the leakage audit in Section 5.2 (per-fold rebuild, per-fold transition prior, the 13-27 point inflation confession) is exemplary and preempts the obvious attack on a transductively trained representation.
- The narrative architecture is sound and inviting: abstract and introduction give context and tension before any number; the two-act structure (representation makes the task learnable, then one model serves both tasks) is clean; each results subsection opens with a read-this-as sentence; and the Section 4.2 cost paragraph (the joint model is LARGER than the two dedicated models combined, the win is operational, not arithmetic) is the kind of honesty that builds trust in everything else.
- Evaluation breadth with matched protocols: six datasets on two continents, dedicated single-task ceilings tuned per dataset as the like-for-like anchor, external baselines organized into three explicit roles (state of the art, representation controls, coupling topology), and the cascade tested inside the authors' own model rather than as a strawman re-implementation.

**Weaknesses:**
- The mechanism story that makes the headline believable is homeless: the per-place-averaged control ("64 to 90 percent of the gain", the second sentence of contribution 1), the embedding geometry (silhouette 0.57 vs 0.00, purity 0.98 vs 0.78, after Fig. 3 was cut), the freeze-region control ("within 0.3"), and the cascade tie ("a quarter of a point") all live only in prose, with no table or figure a reader can inspect. Tables II and III anchor the headline; nothing anchors the why.
- Empirical findings are reported inside Background and Related Work (Section 2.3): the gradient-balancer negative result and the near-zero gradient-correlation mechanism claim are stated there and never appear in Section 6 with a number. A related-work section positions the literature; it does not carry results that exist nowhere else in the paper.
- The conclusion violates its contract: half of a nine-line conclusion is repository and dataset URLs, and the paper ends on a figshare link rather than a message. The anticipatory-services motivation that opens the abstract and Section 1 is never returned to; there is no forward look.
- New quantitative measurements debut in the Discussion (shortlist compactness 3-8 km, near-miss distances, the 20-241 km random-pair floor, with their own single-seed disclosure) without ever passing through Results; motivation-sketch framing does not exempt measured numbers from having a home.
- The paper is not self-contained on the main model: no trunk dimensions, attention heads, optimizer, learning rate, or epoch budget appear anywhere in Sections 4-5; everything is deferred to the released code. One short implementation paragraph would fix this.
- The abstract's final third collapses under compression ("with the two largest states at a single random initialization" is a fragment no first-time reader can parse), and Section 5.4 is a single roughly 600-word paragraph carrying six systems in three roles.

**Detailed comments:**
- [minor] (Abstract) The contract is honored: context and motivation come before any result, and no model name leads. But the last three sentences are compressed past legibility. "Across the five U.S. states, the gain on region grows with the number of regions, with the two largest states at a single random initialization" reads as a broken sentence; the provisional-seed caveat deserves its own short clause ("the two largest states are measured at a single seed and are provisional"), not a dangling prepositional phrase. Rewrite the tail at half the density; the abstract is the one place where every sentence must parse on first contact.
- [minor] (Section 1 (Introduction)) The arc (hook, tasks, tension, approach, contributions, roadmap) is right, and I note with approval that every contribution bullet is anchored to a specific table or figure. Contribution 3, however, tries to be both a claim and a caveats registry; by its end the reader has absorbed TOST, pp, monotonicity, Istanbul, and the seed accounting in one breath. Keep the bullet to the scaling message and let the seed disclosure be one sentence after the list.
- [major] (Section 2.3 (Related work / MTL)) This subsection reports experiments: "We confirm this: none of the balancers that we tried... improved on a tuned fixed task weighting" and "the next-category and next-region updates on the shared trunk are near zero-correlated throughout training." Neither claim appears in Section 6 with a number, a table, or a figure; the pointer to the "mechanism test in Section 6.2" resolves to the freeze-region control, which is a different experiment. Related work should position the literature and promise the test; the finding itself, with at least one correlation number, belongs in Section 6.2. As written, two empirical claims exist only inside the related-work section, which is the wrong home and makes them unverifiable.
- [minor] (Section 3 (Problem and Tasks)) This section honors its contract well: it states the problem (windows, two targets, label-set sizes) without smuggling in the solution, and the right-sizing of the venue bridge (a census tract is a neighborhood, not a radio cell; no handover claims) is exactly the honesty this audience needs. My only note: the forward promise that Section 7 "quantifies what these predictions would give such a service" sets up the misplacement I flag at Section 7.
- [minor] (Section 4 (Method)) The prose-only, equation-free description is a defensible choice for this audience, and the cost paragraph (4.2M vs 1.1M parameters at Alabama; the win is operational, not arithmetic) is the best paragraph in the section. But the paper never states the trunk width, number of attention heads, optimizer, learning rate, or epoch budget, in this section or in Section 5; everything is deferred to the repository. A single implementation paragraph in Section 5 would make the paper self-contained; a paper should not require a git clone to answer "how big is the trunk?".
- [minor] (Section 5 (Experimental Setup)) Section 5.2 is the strongest setup subsection I have read in this literature in some time; the per-fold transition-prior confession (13 to 27 points of inflation in an earlier version) buys enormous credibility. Section 5.4, by contrast, is one monolithic paragraph carrying three roles and six systems; the three-role structure is already present in the topic sentences, so give each role its own paragraph and the reader a place to breathe.

## Review 8: Graph representation-learning researcher (infomax methods: DGI/HGI lineage, GraphSAGE-style inductive encoders; contextual location embeddings: CTLE/TALE), reviewing for the intersection of GRL and spatiotemporal mobility.

**Score: 4 (weak accept)** — A methodologically honest, statistically careful two-part empirical study whose headline representation gain is probably real but whose core construction is under-specified and whose attribution controls are reported without numbers; weak accept for this venue.

**Summary:** The paper studies two coarse next-visit prediction tasks on LBSN check-ins, next-category (7 classes) and next-region (520 to 8,501 census tracts / mahalle), across five Gowalla states and Istanbul. Part 1 extends the DGI-to-HGI hierarchical infomax lineage one level below the place, to individual check-ins, producing one 64-d vector per visit (with category, time-of-day, and day-of-week as node input features), and reports +28 to +40 macro-F1 on next-category over a place-level HGI embedding under a matched protocol; a per-place-averaged control and a feature-concat control are invoked to attribute most of the gain to per-visit context. Part 2 trains a single dual-stream cross-attention model that predicts both targets in one forward pass and reports it outperforming per-dataset-tuned dedicated category models everywhere (+5.3 to +9.4) and dedicated region models at four of six datasets, with TOST non-inferiority at the other two, plus a same-model cascade rewiring that ties the parallel coupling. The statistical apparatus (paired tests with Holm, TOST with a justified 2 pp margin, provisional single-seed cells flagged, a per-fold-rebuild transductivity audit at three states) is unusually careful for this literature.

**Strengths:**
- Statistical honesty well above the norm for mobility-prediction papers: pre-specified superiority vs non-inferiority verbs bound to their tests (paired t + Holm; TOST with an a-priori, deployment-justified 2 pp margin and a power statement), the Alabama region deficit reported as a significant deficit rather than hidden, CA/TX single-seed cells explicitly marked provisional in the abstract, contributions, table, and limitations.
- The paper identifies exactly the right confounds for its own headline and probes each one: a per-fold train-users-only rebuild of the representation (leakage-through-transduction), a per-place-averaged version (context vs extra training signal), a feature-concat control (feature injection), an acknowledged CTLE contrast (contextualization without category vocabulary), and a quantified 13-27 pp region-transition-prior leak that was found and removed from all of its own models.
- Breadth and consistency of the empirical claim: six datasets spanning two continents, two region ontologies, and a 16x range in region count under one protocol, with an honest cost accounting (the joint model is ~2-4x the parameters of the two dedicated models combined; the claimed benefit is operational, not arithmetic) and a same-model cascade comparison that is framed as a tie, not a win.

**Weaknesses:**
- The core technical contribution, the check-in-level infomax, is not specified to a reproducible level in the text: no corruption/negative-sampling function, no statement of which MI pairs are contrasted or what the readout/summary returns at each of the four levels, no encoder architecture, and the anchor embedding in the auxiliary loss is unnamed. A GRL reader cannot tell from the paper what 'extending infomax one level deeper' concretely means beyond the graph having a new level.
- The inference path for an unseen check-in is never explained. Whether a new visit gets a vector via an inductive forward pass of the trained encoder or requires graph retraining is load-bearing for a paper motivated by anticipatory services, and the per-fold-rebuild audit implies some inductive mechanism exists (test users' visits must be scored somehow), but the method section is silent.
- The headline attribution ('most of the +28-40 gain comes from per-visit context') rests on controls reported without numbers: the per-place-averaged control is one sentence with an unanchored 'roughly 64 to 90 percent, state-dependent' range and no per-dataset values, and the feature-concat control has no number at all.
- Label proximity is only partially confronted. With category as a node input feature, the reported kNN category purity of 0.98 says the per-visit vectors nearly encode each past visit's category; 'training never sees the next category' rules out leakage (past categories are legitimately observable at inference) but does not rule out that the gap over HGI and CTLE is mostly category-feature availability rather than the hierarchical-graph-infomax construction. The 2x2 attribution design is missing exactly the cell that would test the combination claim: a contextual non-graph embedding WITH category features (e.g., CTLE with categories appended), since the feature-concat control pairs categories with the static embedding only.
- The CTLE comparison is weak evidence even as acknowledged: end-to-end at one dataset at author defaults, where it lands below even the static place embedding (a strong sign of under-adaptation rather than an inherent limit), frozen at three others, absent at TX/CA.
- The transductivity audit covers 3 of 6 datasets, its category axis is a place-level proxy on 67-87% of visits, and the two provisional single-seed states (CA/TX) that anchor the top of the region-scaling trend are exactly the ones not audited; the scaling narrative itself is confounded with corpus size (acknowledged, but it still headlines the abstract and Fig. 4).

## Review 10: TPC meta-reviewer, MobiWac 2026 (networking-systems committee, ~26% acceptance)

**Score: 3 (borderline)** — Rigor far above venue norm carries a paper whose network-facing consequence is still a promise; borderline as submitted, flipping to accept with one measured service-proxy experiment and the CA/TX seeds.

**Summary:** The paper studies joint prediction of two coarse properties of a user's next check-in, its category (seven classes) and its region (census tract or mahalle, 520 to 8,501 classes), across five Gowalla states and Istanbul. Part 1 introduces Check2HGI, which extends hierarchical graph infomax one level down so each visit gets its own contextual vector, lifting next-category macro-F1 by +28 to +40 points over a place-level HGI embedding under a matched protocol. Part 2 shows a single cross-attention dual-tower model with a private spatial path that outperforms per-dataset-tuned dedicated category models everywhere (+5.3 to +9.4 macro-F1) and outperforms or TOST-matches dedicated region models, with the region gain rising with region count, though the two largest states are single-seed provisional. Evaluation uses user-disjoint five-fold CV, a train-users-only leakage audit, pre-registered superiority and non-inferiority tests, and external baselines (HMT-GRN, STAN, ReHDM, POI-RGNN, Markov floors). The mobility-management application is explicitly motivation only: the paper builds and measures no network or service decision, offering instead a quantified usage sketch (shortlist coverage and geographic compactness) in the discussion.

**Strengths:**
- Statistical and methodological rigor well above this venue's norm: user-disjoint five-fold CV, a measured transductivity/leakage audit reported with its residual (including the cold-POI proxy caveat and the 13-27 pp transition-prior inflation it caught), pre-registered superiority vs non-inferiority testing with an a-priori justified two-point TOST margin and power analysis, and a checkpoint-selection disclosure showing no claim depends on reading two epochs.
- Unusual reporting honesty that a committee can trust: the joint model's cost is stated against itself (larger than both dedicated models combined), CA/TX cells are flagged provisional in the abstract, intro, table, figure, and discussion, the cascade comparison is reported as a tie rather than a win, and the AL region deficit is called a statistically significant deficit rather than buried.
- Breadth and control quality of the evaluation: six real check-in datasets across two continents and two region ontologies, per-dataset-tuned dedicated ceilings as the comparison bar, representation controls (per-place-averaged variant, CTLE, feature-concat), a mechanism probe (frozen region pathway), and a coupling-topology test against the field's dominant cascade pattern.

**Weaknesses:**
- Venue fit is motivation-only: §3 explicitly disclaims cell association and handover and states no service is built or evaluated; there is no network-facing metric, no reactive-policy comparison, and every headline number is macro-F1 or Acc@10, which is exactly the profile this guide's committee historically scores down.
- The paper's most quotable claim, that the region gain grows with region count, rests its two strongest points (Texas +2.12, California +2.20) on single-seed provisional cells, while the four confirmed datasets show only -0.31 to +0.72; the trend as stated in the abstract outruns the confirmed evidence.
- The operational argument for the single model is thinner than the framing suggests: by the paper's own accounting the joint model is larger than the two dedicated models combined and costs more per forward pass than running both, leaving only the one-artifact/one-input convenience claim, which is asserted rather than measured (no latency, memory, or serving numbers).
- Two load-bearing controls are cited without numbers: the feature-concat control ('does not close the gap') and the per-place-averaged attribution ('roughly 64 to 90 percent') appear only as prose, so the reader cannot verify that the +28 to +40 substrate gap is not substantially a feature-availability artifact (HGI's arm never sees per-visit category or time features).
- Where statistically confirmed, region improvements are small in absolute terms (+0.28 to +0.72 Acc@10, with a significant -0.31 deficit at Alabama), and the region-count trend is confounded with corpus size, which the paper acknowledges but the abstract and Fig. 4 still foreground.
