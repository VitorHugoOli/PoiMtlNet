% S2b · Two models, or one? (90 s) -- NEW. The printed research question (point 1), the MTL appeal
% and risk (point 8: never "known to fail", never "one cost"), the literature gap (point 6: never
% "first", never "few MTL models"; scoped exactly as 02_related "fine-grained region as an end target
% of equal standing"), and the short answer at minute 2 (point 9).
\begin{frame}{Two models, or one?}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.2ex}
        \item \textbf{Two dedicated models}: one per question; two models to train, version and serve.
        \item \textbf{One joint model}: one artifact to deploy, and the tasks might help each other.
              \alert{But shared parameters can settle on a compromise good for neither task}
              \scite{Caruana 1997}.
    \end{itemize}

    \smallskip
    {\footnotesize Multi-task models in this field use category and region as auxiliary signals for
    next-place prediction \scite{MCMG, Sun 2024; HMT-GRN, Lim 2022}. To our knowledge, fine-grained
    region as an end target of equal standing is underexplored.\par}

    \smallskip
    \begin{alertblock}{The question}
        \centering
        Can one model predict both the category and the region of a user's next visit,
        and what does it cost?\par
        \smallskip{\footnotesize\textbf{Short answer: yes, at a measured price.}}
    \end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

            \footnotesize
            \textbf{Multi-task learning}: one model does several tasks at once by sharing most of
            its parts.\par\vspace{3mm}
            \textbf{\color{alertshade}The risk}: what the tasks share can settle on a compromise
            worse for each.\par\vspace{3mm}

            shared trunk, attention lets each task read the other's features while keeping its
            own weights: \textbf{they share by exchanging information}.\par\vspace{3mm}
            \alert{\textbf{A private spatial path keeps what is purely spatial out of the
            exchange.}}\par

% B2 · "Where do the 2 points come from? Chosen after looking?" Registered vs reported separated
% (Ch.5 05_setup: the plan registered a paired Wilcoxon; at four seeds its exact one-sided p cannot
% go below 0.0625, so a paired t carries the verdict; both tests are in the code release).
\begin{frame}{B2 $\cdot$ The margin, and the tests}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \small
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.4ex}
        \item \textbf{Registered before any result was read}: superiority for category,
              non-inferiority for region with a margin of \textbf{2 Acc@10 points}, paired Wilcoxon;
        \item \textbf{Why 2}: a service that acts on which neighborhood will be busy would not behave
              differently below it; the seed-to-seed sd of the paired difference is 0.02 to 0.16;
        \item \textbf{Reported}: paired $t$ on the four per-seed means, 90\% interval, Holm across
              the six datasets per task; at four seeds the exact Wilcoxon cannot go below 0.0625.
              Both tests are in the code release;
        \item Category has \textbf{no registered equivalence margin}: a difference that fails
              superiority is \alert{unresolved}, never a match;
        \item The two region gains are \textbf{secondary results}, outside the registered plan.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% B3 · "Wouldn't a Markov chain do this?" Only OUR margin over the floor; never "the floor beats the
% externals" (at its best epoch HMT-GRN is above the floor everywhere).
\begin{frame}{B3 $\cdot$ The Markov floor}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.2ex}
        \item A first-order Markov chain over region transitions, no learning:
              \textbf{51.23 to 72.47} Acc@10;
        \item The joint model exceeds it by \textbf{$+4.1$ to $+10.0$} at every dataset; the
              narrowest is \textbf{Florida, $+4.07$}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% B4 · "How do you know a visit's vector does not see the target?" -- published, limit 3.
\begin{frame}{B4 $\cdot$ Can a visit vector see its target?}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.2ex}
        \item Each visit node draws on the \textbf{visits that precede it}: the vector encodes the
              visit with its own history;
        \item The graph does \textbf{not pass information from a later visit back to an earlier
              one}, in training or at readout;
        \item That is what keeps a node from carrying a feature of the target it is used to predict;
        \item The graph is trained without the task labels: it never sees the next category or the
              next region.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% B5 · "What does one model instead of two cost?"
\begin{frame}{B5 $\cdot$ One model instead of two}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.2ex}
        \item \textbf{4.2 M vs 1.9 M} parameters at Alabama, \textbf{5.2 M vs 2.8 M} at California,
              against \textbf{the two dedicated models combined};
        \item One pass costs more compute than running the two small models;
        \item The gain is \alert{operational}: one artifact to train, version and deploy; one pass
              returns both answers.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% B6 · "If the representation does most of the work, what is multi-task learning for?"
\begin{frame}{B6 $\cdot$ Then what is multi-task learning for?}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.2ex}
        \item On category, the representation moves the score \textbf{more than the choice between
              one model and two};
        \item Whether the exchange between the tasks adds anything beyond the representation is
              \alert{not separated by the evidence here};
        \item What one model gives for certain is operational: one artifact, two answers.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% B7 · "Why these external systems, and how were they run?"
\begin{frame}{B7 $\cdot$ The external systems}
    \vspace{0pt}% guard
    \small
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.4ex}
        \item \textbf{HMT-GRN} \scite{Lim 2022} --- primary region comparison: our windows and folds,
              one seed, best validation epoch; its multi-task structure kept, the graph components and
              hierarchical beam search removed, a per-fold region-transition prior added (our own
              reported models do not use it); not the complete published system;
        \item \textbf{STAN} \scite{Luo 2021} --- re-implemented with \alert{its own representations
              and splits} from the raw check-ins, output adapted to rank regions, seed 0;
        \item \textbf{ReHDM} \scite{Li 2025} --- its own published chronological split;
              \alert{single seed at TX and CA};
        \item \textbf{POI-RGNN} \scite{Capanema 2023} --- next category, re-implemented,
              non-overlapping windows.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
